# How can readers and AI systems find your public work?

Publish useful, attributable answers on accessible public pages, connect them to maintained sources, and measure actual discovery separately from technical access.

## The short answer

Make a useful public explanation easy to read, attribute, and reach. Name a real question, answer it clearly, show an example and limitations, and link to the supporting sources. These practices help people use and check your work; they do not guarantee search indexing or AI citations.

## How it works

For a public website, use descriptive titles, a canonical address, readable rendered text, working links, compatible crawl and index directives, and a sitemap containing only intended public pages. Structured data should describe what a reader can actually see. A short machine-readable index can be convenient without being a special ranking requirement.

For a public GitHub companion, make the first useful task easy to find. Identify the author, title, appropriate version, licensing, sources and correction path. Citation instructions help someone attribute the resource; they are not proof that an assistant will cite it.

## A constructed example

An author has a private manuscript and a public verification worksheet. A short public reference explains how to match a completion claim to evidence and links to the worksheet and book chapter. The public page gives a reader something useful without exposing the private manuscript or claiming that the worksheet certifies production readiness.

## When to use it

Apply these checks to intended public articles, documentation, course pages, companion repositories and product resources. Keep confidential drafts, internal systems and restricted records outside the public corpus. A discovery goal does not authorize removing their access controls or changing training permissions.

## What to watch for

- A page that is public to a browser but blocked from indexing or required assets.
- Structured data that invents reviews, results, authority or qualifications.
- A copied explanation drifting away from the source that owns it.
- Keyword repetition or many thin pages replacing a useful answer.
- Treating a successful HTTP request as proof of indexing, citation or commercial value.

## Try it

Choose one resource you intend to publish. Check its answer, byline, date or version, source links, license and correction route. If it is a website, check the actual public response, canonical URL, rendered content, robots directives and sitemap entry. Keep private routes protected while testing.

## Measure distinct outcomes

Record public access, observed indexing, actual assistant citations, reader usefulness, learning and commercial outcomes separately. For a citation observation, retain the date, provider or model, exact question, settings and cited URL. If nothing has been observed yet, say so. Do not claim a gain from technical checks alone.
